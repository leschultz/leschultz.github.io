01 / Research

Materials questions.
Computational answers.

My work connects learning algorithms with physical models, from molecular separations and battery electrolytes to the growth of thin films.

01Molecular separations

Learning which frameworks to explore next.

Metal–organic frameworks offer a vast space of porous materials. I combine graph neural networks, uncertainty estimates, and multi-objective Bayesian optimization to screen for favorable separation performance.

Graph neural networks · Bayesian optimization

Published · 2026Paper details ↗
02Solid-state batteries

Finding useful tradeoffs in electrolyte microstructures.

A candidate pool of 174,758 LLZO–polymer microstructures provides a testing ground for surrogate-guided screening. The work connects transport and mechanical tradeoffs with interpretable microstructural features.

Multi-objective optimization · Microstructure

In preparation · 2026Manuscript details ↗
03Thin-film modeling

Bringing atomic detail into sputtering predictions.

Surface binding energies vary with chemistry and structure. I investigate how atomistically resolved distributions in Mo–S systems propagate through sputtering and deposition models, with experimental sputter yields as a benchmark.

Atomistic simulation · Multiscale modeling

Under review · 2026Manuscript details ↗

Research foundations

Knowing when to trust a prediction.

My doctoral research developed applicability-domain methods for materials machine learning and high-throughput atomistic workflows for metallic glass forming ability. These questions—what a model knows, and what a simulation reveals—continue to guide my work.

03 / Background

A computational perspective.
An engineering foundation.

I’m a materials scientist and engineer at the Center for Integrated Nanotechnologies at Sandia National Laboratories. My work brings together materials physics, scientific computing, and machine learning.

I earned my Ph.D. at the University of Wisconsin–Madison and my engineering degree at Fort Lewis College. Alongside research, I’ve helped build and administer scientific computing clusters and supported students and colleagues in using research software.
